A cracked screen on the way to work, a battery that drops from 40% to zero, or a phone that has met a rainy pavement can throw off your whole day. This phone repair checklist helps you take the right steps before handing your device over, protect what matters and give the technician the clearest possible picture of the fault.

You do not need to diagnose the problem yourself. In fact, guessing can make things more confusing. A few quick checks are enough to show whether the issue is urgent, whether your data is safe and what information will help get your repair moving quickly.

Start with safety, not the repair

If your phone is swollen, unusually hot, smoking, giving off a chemical smell or has a visibly lifted screen, stop using it. These can be signs of a damaged battery. Do not charge it, press the screen back into place or leave it under a pillow, in a bag or in direct sunlight. Keep it on a non-flammable surface and arrange professional help as soon as possible.

A severely cracked screen also needs care. Small cracks may only be inconvenient, but loose glass can cut your fingers and allow moisture or dust inside the handset. If the display is flickering, showing black patches or responding unpredictably, avoid pressing hard on the damaged area.

Water damage is another case where speed matters. Switch the phone off if you can, remove the case and take out the SIM tray. Gently dry the outside with a clean cloth. Do not charge it, use a hairdryer or put it in rice. Rice does not remove moisture from inside the phone and loose grains can create another problem. A proper inspection is the safer option, especially if the phone is needed for work, travel or banking.

Your phone repair checklist for common faults

Before booking a repair, take two minutes to notice exactly what is and is not working. This does not replace a technician's diagnosis, but it can save time at the counter and help establish whether one fault has caused another.

For a damaged screen, check whether the display lights up, whether touch works across the whole panel and whether the front camera still focuses. Look for lines, dead areas, colour changes and black ink-like patches. A phone can have intact glass but a failed display underneath, so describe both the appearance and how it behaves.

For battery concerns, think about the pattern. Does the phone drain quickly all day, shut down in cold weather, only charge at a certain angle or become hot while charging? Also try a known working cable and plug once. A worn cable is a simpler fix than a faulty charging port, although repeated connection problems, debris in the port or a loose socket should still be checked professionally.

If sound is the issue, test a call on speakerphone, play a video and make a short voice recording. This can help distinguish between a speaker, microphone, earpiece or software problem. Similarly, if the camera is blurry, check whether the lens is cracked or covered by a protector, then test both front and rear cameras in good light.

Make a short note of the following before you visit:

  • when the problem first appeared and whether it followed a drop, spill or update
  • what the phone does now, including error messages, flickering or unexpected restarts
  • which features still work, such as calls, Wi-Fi, Face ID, fingerprint login, cameras and charging
  • whether the device has been repaired before or has non-original parts fitted
  • the make, model and storage capacity, if you know them

Clear details are more useful than a technical explanation. “It charges for five minutes, then stops” is far more helpful than “the battery is broken”.

Protect your data before a repair

Most screen, battery, camera and charging repairs do not require your personal data to be accessed. Even so, a backup is sensible whenever your phone still switches on. Back up to your preferred cloud account or a computer, then check that recent photos, contacts and messages have actually completed syncing. A backup that has not finished is not much comfort after a major fault.

If your screen is working, remove sensitive items you do not need to carry around, such as paper notes containing passwords. Do not share your passcode, banking PIN or account passwords with anyone. A reputable repair service should explain if access is genuinely needed for testing and should handle your device with care.

You may need to turn off a device-tracking or activation feature before certain repairs can be completed. This varies by manufacturer and repair type. Do this only when you are with the repair technician or when you understand why it is required. Turning it off too early makes a lost phone less secure.

It is also worth checking your eSIM or physical SIM arrangement. A standard repair should not erase your mobile number, but knowing your network login details and keeping the SIM safe can prevent unnecessary disruption. If the phone cannot be used at all, have a way to receive important calls or authentication codes while it is being repaired.

Prepare the phone for a faster handover

Bring the handset itself and, if the issue relates to charging, bring the cable and plug you normally use. There is no need to bring the original box unless the technician asks for it. Remove bulky cases, card holders and accessories that may hide damage or make inspection harder.

If possible, charge the phone to at least 20%. A little power allows the technician to test the display, cameras, sound and charging behaviour before and after the work. When a phone will not power on, do not worry - just explain what happened immediately before it stopped working.

Take photographs of the device before handing it in, particularly if there are existing dents, scratches or cracks around the frame and rear glass. This is not about expecting a problem. It simply gives everyone a clear record of the phone's condition and helps avoid confusion later.

If time is tight, book ahead where available. For central London commuters, a repair near Piccadilly Circus Underground Station can be easier to fit around a workday than travelling across town with a failing phone. Many common faults can be assessed quickly, but turnaround depends on the model, the damage, the availability of suitable parts and whether hidden damage is found during inspection.

Ask the questions that make pricing clear

A good repair quote should be understandable. Ask what fault is being repaired, whether the price includes parts and labour, and whether the part is compatible with your particular phone model. If you have a newer handset, a foldable phone or a device with advanced face or fingerprint features, the right part and careful fitting matter even more.

It is reasonable to ask about the expected turnaround, repair warranty and what happens if inspection reveals additional damage. Water exposure, for example, can affect more than one component. A technician may be able to repair the immediate fault, but should be honest if corrosion or board damage creates a risk of later issues.

The cheapest quote is not always the best value. Poor-quality parts can affect brightness, touch response, battery life, camera performance or durability. On the other hand, not every older phone justifies an expensive repair. The sensible choice depends on the device's age, condition, value and how well it meets your needs after the repair.

Check the repair before you leave

Once the phone is returned, take a few minutes to test it while you are still in the shop. Check the screen brightness and touch response, make a call, test the cameras, play audio and connect a charger. If biometrics were working before the repair, test those too.

For a battery replacement, it may take a few charge cycles before you get a clear picture of day-to-day performance. For a screen replacement, keep a screen protector and case in mind once the repair is complete. They cannot prevent every accident, but they can make the next drop less costly.

A damaged phone is stressful because it carries so much of your life. A calm, practical check before repair gives you better answers, fewer surprises and the best chance of getting back to normal quickly.
