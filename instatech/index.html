Critical findings

Each of these materially limits whether the business is found in Google or recommended by an AI assistant.

Critical

GEO: the brand has almost no presence off its own website

Web searches for “InstaTech phone repair London Marchmont Street reviews” and for the literal domain "instatech.uk" return no InstaTech result: no review profile, directory listing or press mention. Category prompts (“best phone repair shop Bloomsbury”, “phone screen repair Moorgate Old Street”) surface iSmash Tottenham Court Road and Square Repair instead. ChatGPT search runs largely on Bing's index, and Perplexity, Copilot and AI Overviews all favour corroborating third-party sources. A shop that exists only on its own domain is therefore rarely named, however good that site is. The schema's sameAs lists only TikTok and two Google Maps links.

Fix: (1) Fully complete Google Business Profiles for both shops, with categories, services, prices, photos and a review-request routine. (2) Claim Bing Places and Apple Business Connect: these feed ChatGPT/Copilot and Siri/Apple Maps. (3) Build consistent NAP citations on Trustpilot, Yelp, Yell, Thomson Local, FreeIndex, Checkatrade and Cylex. (4) Pursue 3–5 local editorial mentions (Bloomsbury/Clerkenwell/City local press, the UCL/SOAS student guides near Marchmont Street). (5) Add every resulting profile URL to Organization and LocalBusiness sameAs.

Critical

Mobile load is very slow: LCP ~10s, main thread blocked ~3s

Homepage: performance 27, FCP 10.0s, LCP 10.1s, TBT 2,990ms, 2.2 MB transferred, 2,035 DOM nodes, 44 scripts. One homepage request recorded a 2.5s server response (a cache miss; a cached hit measured 0.6s). Third-party code blocked the main thread for 290ms. LCP is a ranking input, and slow first paint also cuts walk-in conversions from “near me” mobile searches.

Fix: In LiteSpeed Cache, enable JS defer/delay for non-critical scripts, CSS critical-path generation and unused-CSS removal. Dequeue WooCommerce cart/fragment scripts on non-shop pages. Preload the hero image with fetchpriority="high". Reduce page-builder DOM depth on the homepage. Re-test in PageSpeed Insights and use its field data as the measure of success.

Critical

The homepage H1 is a promotional banner, not what the business does

The homepage's only H1 is 2 shops weren’t enough. Oxford Circus is next. The page's main heading, which search engines and AI extractors weight most heavily when deciding what a page is about, therefore carries no service (“phone repair”) and no location. The title tag (“Phone & Laptop Repair London – Same Day”) is well targeted, but the H1 contradicts it.

Fix: Make the H1 something like “Phone, laptop & tablet repair in central London, same day” and demote the Oxford Circus announcement to an H2 or a banner.

High-priority findings

These weaken entity clarity and trust: the signals AI systems use to decide whom to cite.

High

The brand name is spelt two ways: “InstaTech” and “instaTech”

Most titles and the Organization schema use InstaTech. The Moorgate, Bloomsbury, Oxford Circus and screen-repair titles, the Service schema's provider names and the Google Maps listing names use instaTech. Entity-matching systems treat consistent naming as a confidence signal when linking a website, a Maps listing and third-party mentions into one business.

Fix: Choose one form and apply it everywhere: titles, schema, both GBP listings, social handles and citations.

High

The LocalBusiness schema is present but underspecified

Both shops' url points to /locations/ rather than their own location pages. hasMap uses generic Maps search URLs with no place ID or CID. image is the logo file (WhatsApp-Image-2026-05-07…png) rather than a shopfront photo. Organization has no sameAs, contactPoint or foundingDate. The third shop (22 Great Castle St, W1, opening October 2026) is missing from the schema and from llms.txt.

Fix: Point each url at its location page. Use the GBP share link containing the CID for hasMap. Add shopfront photos and sameAs profiles. Add the Oxford Circus location to the schema and llms.txt once it is trading.

High

The blog author entity is empty on 60 posts

Every BlogPosting references #author, but that Person node has no name, URL or credentials. Repair-advice content (“are third-party repairs safe”, “iPhone unknown part message”) is the kind of content where demonstrable hands-on expertise decides whether Google and AI answers trust it.

Fix: Publish a named technician author with a bio page (years repairing, device specialisms, shop location). Give the Person node a name, url, jobTitle and worksFor. Add first-hand repair photos to posts.

High

Blog volume and spread carry a scaled-content risk

61 posts of a uniform ~1,800–3,100 words, 36 of them showing the same last-modified date (13 Sep 2026). Roughly a third are generic affiliate-style buying guides (“best power banks for students”, “best phone mounts for cars”) that are unrelated to the core repair intent. A theme demo page, /blog-standard/, is indexed, has no H1 and is in the sitemap.

Fix: Noindex or delete /blog-standard/. Consolidate the accessory guides into fewer, stronger pages or retire them. Put effort into repair-intent pages that carry original evidence: real prices, turnaround times, before/after photos and London-specific detail. Those are the passages AI answers quote.

Medium-priority findings

Hygiene and accessibility items, best batched into one maintenance pass.

Medium

Shop: 26 of 159 products have no meta description; 5 product title pairs and 6 product descriptions are duplicated

This includes the Lightning splitter, travel adapter, USB-C HDMI and QUATRE multi-port variants. 8 product titles exceed 60 characters.

Medium

Archives: 33 of 33 product categories and 5 of 6 blog categories have no meta description; blog categories have no H1

An empty /category/uncategorized/ archive is indexed. Set it to noindex, or write a short intro for each archive that is worth keeping.

Medium

972 of 3,449 images (28%) have no alt text

Mostly product gallery images (791 of the 972). Core pages are close to complete, with one missing per page, which is likely a shared theme element.

Medium

Accessibility: Lighthouse scores 82 (home) and 89 (screen repair)

Failing checks: colour contrast, touch-target size, focusable elements inside aria-hidden, out-of-order headings, and a non-crawlable <a class="search_close"> anchor with no href.

Medium

The currency-exchange service muddies the topical entity

/currency-exchange-service/ (1,669 words) sits in a site that otherwise covers only device repair and trade. If it is a real in-shop service, keep it but keep it out of the main repair navigation and schema, so that the business stays classified as a repair shop.

Medium

The tablet-repair title targets Bloomsbury only

“Tablet & iPad Repair in Bloomsbury, London WC1” excludes the Moorgate shop, unlike the phone, laptop and watch pages, which target London as a whole.

Medium

No Google or Bing verification tag is visible; IndexNow is not confirmed

Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools may be verified by DNS, which this audit cannot see. Confirm both properties exist and submit the sitemap in Bing. Rank Math can ping IndexNow, which gets new pages into Bing (and so into ChatGPT search) within hours.

Medium

llms.txt could carry more answerable facts

It currently lists locations and links. Add a short price table (screen, battery and charging-port repairs by model family), typical turnaround times, warranty terms, brands covered and the Oxford Circus opening date: the facts an assistant needs to answer “how much / how long / where”.

Where to start

  1. This week: change the homepage H1; noindex /blog-standard/ and /category/uncategorized/; standardise the brand spelling.
  2. This week: claim and complete Bing Places and Apple Business Connect for both shops; audit both Google Business Profiles; start asking every completed repair for a review.
  3. Next 2 weeks: tighten the LocalBusiness and Organization schema (location URLs, Maps CIDs, shopfront images, sameAs) and add a named technician author.
  4. Next 2 weeks: a LiteSpeed Cache performance pass (JS delay, critical CSS, WooCommerce script dequeue, LCP preload), then re-measure in PageSpeed Insights.
  5. Next 60 days: 15–20 consistent NAP citations plus 3–5 local editorial mentions; prune or merge the accessory buying guides; enrich llms.txt with prices and turnaround times.
  6. Ongoing: each month, test 10–15 fixed prompts (“phone repair near Russell Square”, “iPhone screen repair Old Street price”…) in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode, and log whether InstaTech is named.

Method & limitations

All 276 URLs in the Rank Math sitemap index (17 pages, 61 posts, 159 products, 33 product categories, 6 blog categories) were fetched and parsed from the live site on 21 October 2026. Every on-page count comes from that parse. AI-crawler access was tested by requesting a service page with each crawler's user agent. Performance comes from one local Lighthouse 12 mobile run per page (homepage and screen repair). The run used constrained cloud hardware, so treat absolute values as indicative. PageSpeed Insights, and with it Google's real-user field data, was unavailable (daily API quota exhausted). The off-site footprint was assessed with web searches for brand, domain and category-plus-locality queries. That is a proxy, not a full citation or backlink audit. Not available in this run: Ahrefs backlink and AI-citation data (the connected plan does not include these endpoints), Semrush keyword and traffic data (the account had insufficient API units), and Google Search Console (the connector failed to connect). Google Business Profile content and review counts were not inspected.
