What it means when a semen analysis finds no sperm, the two main types, how it is diagnosed and the treatment routes to parenthood.

In short

  • Azoospermia means there is no measurable sperm in the ejaculate.
  • It affects about 1% of all men and around 10–15% of men with infertility.
  • There are two main types, obstructive and non-obstructive, and they are treated differently.
  • Many men still have options, including sperm retrieval with ICSI or donor sperm. A urologist or andrologist can talk them through with you.

What is azoospermia?

Azoospermia means there is no measurable sperm in the ejaculate. It affects about 1% of all men and around 10–15% of men with infertility.

It is usually confirmed by at least two semen analyses, with the sample spun down (centrifuged) so the lab can look carefully for any sperm at all.

The two main types

Blockage

Obstructive

Sperm are made, but a blockage stops them reaching the ejaculate. This can happen:

  • after a vasectomy
  • after infection or injury
  • with congenital absence of the vas deferens, which is linked to cystic fibrosis gene changes
Production

Non-obstructive

The testes make very little or no sperm. Causes include:

  • genetic conditions such as Klinefelter syndrome or Y-chromosome microdeletions
  • hormone problems
  • a history of undescended testes
  • previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy

How it is diagnosed

Working out which type you have shapes every next step. Your doctor may arrange:

  • Repeat semen analysisTo confirm the result on a second sample.
  • Physical examinationOf the testes and the tubes that carry sperm.
  • Hormone blood testsFSH, LH and testosterone.
  • Genetic testsKaryotype, Y-chromosome microdeletion and CFTR.
  • Scrotal ultrasoundSometimes, to look at the testes and surrounding structures.

Treatment options

If it is obstructive

Surgical repair, such as a vasectomy reversal, or sperm retrieval (PESA, MESA or TESA) with the sperm then used in ICSI.

If it is non-obstructive

In some men, hormone treatment. Surgical sperm retrieval, such as micro-TESE, with any sperm found used in ICSI.

Donor sperm

Donor sperm is another route to parenthood, and one many couples on the forum have talked through openly.

Talk your options through with a specialist. A urologist or andrologist can explain which routes fit your results.
