Azoospermia

Azoospermia means there is no measurable sperm in the ejaculate. It is a diagnosis many men have never heard of until it is theirs. This guide explains the types, the tests and the routes to parenthood.

In short

  • Azoospermia means no measurable sperm in the ejaculate. It affects about 1% of all men and around 10–15% of men with infertility.
  • There are two main types: obstructive (sperm are made but blocked) and non-obstructive (the testes make very little or no sperm).
  • Diagnosis uses repeat semen analysis, an examination, hormone and genetic blood tests, and sometimes an ultrasound scan.
  • Depending on the type, options include surgical repair, sperm retrieval used with ICSI, hormone treatment in some men, or donor sperm.

What is azoospermia?

Azoospermia is the medical term for having no measurable sperm in the ejaculate. It affects about 1% of all men, and around 10–15% of men with infertility.

It is usually confirmed by at least two semen analyses. The laboratory spins the sample down (centrifuges it) to look closely for any sperm at all before the result is called azoospermia.

Key facts
What it meansNo measurable sperm in the ejaculate
How commonAbout 1% of all men; around 10–15% of men with infertility
How it is confirmedAt least two semen analyses, with the sample centrifuged
Main typesObstructive and non-obstructive
Who to talk toA urologist or andrologist

The two main types

Knowing which type you have matters, because it shapes which treatment options are open to you.

Type 1

Obstructive azoospermia

Sperm are being made, but a blockage stops them reaching the ejaculate.

Causes include

  • A previous vasectomy
  • Infection or injury
  • Congenital absence of the vas deferens, which is linked to cystic fibrosis gene changes
Type 2

Non-obstructive azoospermia

The testes make very little or no sperm.

Causes include

  • Genetic conditions such as Klinefelter syndrome or Y-chromosome microdeletions
  • Hormone problems
  • A history of undescended testes
  • Previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy

How it is diagnosed

Your doctor will usually build up a picture from several tests. You may hear these terms at the clinic:

  • Repeat semen analysisTo confirm that no sperm are present, including in the centrifuged sample.
  • Physical examinationA check by a doctor, often a urologist or andrologist.
  • Hormone blood testsFSH, LH and testosterone.
  • Genetic testsKaryotype, Y-chromosome microdeletion and CFTR (cystic fibrosis gene) testing.
  • Scrotal ultrasoundSometimes used to look at the testes and surrounding structures.

Treatment options

The options depend on the type of azoospermia and its cause.

Options by type
TypeOptions
ObstructiveSurgical repair, such as vasectomy reversal; or sperm retrieval (PESA, MESA, TESA) used with ICSI
Non-obstructiveIn some men, hormone treatment; surgical sperm retrieval such as micro-TESE, used with ICSI
Either typeDonor sperm is another route to parenthood

Talk your options through with a urologist or andrologist. They can explain what your own test results mean for you.
